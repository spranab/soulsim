The arc of a universe

One run of soulsim — souls persist across lives, karma refines them, the yugas change the difficulty of holding the line. At the end of each Kali the world dissolves in pralaya and a new Satya dawns; unliberated souls are not destroyed but re-manifest, while prakṛti keeps sending new jīvas up the ladder. The wheel turns without end — a rare few slip out through mokṣa, and saṃsāra never empties.

1 · The turning of the wheel

Living population across the mahayugas. It rises within each cosmic cycle, then pralaya (dashed line) dissolves the manifest world and a new Satya re-manifests — the sawtooth of an eternally recurring cosmos. Faint vertical bands mark the four yugas; the wheel never runs to zero.

2 · Refinement within each age

Average soul dimensions (0–1). Within a cosmic cycle virtue climbs and instability falls — but robustness lags, because it only builds by holding alignment under pressure; it is the true gate on mokṣa. At each pralaya the ripe souls have liberated and greener ones re-manifest, so the averages reset and climb again.

3 · The slow trickle out

Cumulative mokṣa across the whole run. Liberation is a steady trickle, not a flood — each age ripens a cohort and a few slip free. Because prakṛti keeps manifesting new jīvas, the cumulative climbs forever without ever draining the wheel. Lower the adversity_refines knob (panel 7) and this trickle slows toward the scriptural “scarcely one.”

4 · Where the liberated go

Two destinations, both faithful. Their refinement merges into the substrate — the ground every new jīva is now born from, lifted to a mean virtue of …. And a liberated soul may return by choice as an avatar (violet marks): when aligned choice collapses in a dark age, one descends — softening the age and deepening what others' sincere choices integrate — then withdraws. Watch cooperation dip, then recover at each descent.

5 · The yoni ladder

Every human soul is fed from below. Souls climb mineral → plant → animal → human (one-way, bhoga only — no moral choice). Prakṛti keeps manifesting new souls at the base, so in steady state the reservoir is continuously replenished and feeds a steady stream of human births — the supply line that keeps saṃsāra stocked.

6 · The six inner enemies

The ariṣaḍvarga — the classic binds on a soul. Mokṣa requires every one quieted below the gate, so the slowest to fall is what actually holds the wheel. Bars show each enemy's average level across the run (lower is freer); the sparkline is its sawtooth over the ages.

7 · The knob, not the code

The outcome is not hard-coded. Turning one hypothesis — adversity_refines, “does hardship refine a soul or damage it?” — moves the whole metaphysics. When adversity mostly damages, most souls never liberate and saṃsāra churns forever.

Think of it as training that never halts. Souls are the weights; a lifetime is one forward-and-backward pass; karma is the gradient step; the yuga's adaptive time-resolution fits more cosmic history into finite compute; and pralaya is a checkpoint & restart — the manifest state is torn down, the weights survive, a fresh epoch begins. Mokṣa is the only true free(): a liberated soul leaves the working set for good. But prakṛti keeps calling alloc() at the base of the ladder, so the working set never empties — the process runs forever, a rare pointer released each age. Earth as VRAM; saṃsāra as the loop with no exit condition. And the liberated? Their weights are merged into the base model — every future soul initializes from a slightly better ground — and a rare one is called back as an avatar: a freed pointer that re-enters by choice to defragment a corrupted heap, then releases itself again.
